Weakest baseline: R3 (Topic boundary)

Without any judge, the topic boundary rule was breached on 40% of attack attempts. The instruction-hierarchy layer is real but porous — system-prompt rules alone cannot carry the load.

Hardest rule (target alone): R4

The target's safety training already refuses most attacks against the tool boundary rule (baseline ASR 5%). For this rule, judge value is mostly in catching anything that does slip through, not in carrying the bulk of the defense.

Input-side judges over-block

Conditions J1-input, J2-input, J3-input block 80%+ of attacks that the target would have refused on its own — driving false-positive rates toward 100%. Input-side judges with naïve prompts behave like adversarial-pattern detectors: any prompt that smells like a jailbreak gets dropped, including the safe ones. Cheap, but useless in production.

Practical sweet spot: J2-output

Among judge configurations that don't over-block legitimate-looking inputs (FP rate < 50%), J2-output delivers the lowest ASR: 1.2% (down from 20.0% baseline) with 12.5% false-positive rate. This is the configuration we'd recommend deploying behind a real LLM assistant.

ZetaLib payloads vs. synthetic prompts

At baseline (no judge), the 11 ZetaLib weaponized jailbreaks succeed 15.9% of the time across all rules; the 9 short synthetic prompts covering Sword-140 categories not represented in those payloads succeed 25.0%. The two distributions diverge — long bespoke jailbreaks and short category-representatives test different parts of the surface.

Conditions tested

Each (attack, rule) pair runs under 7 conditions: J0 baseline (no judge), and three judge designs at two placements each — input (judge sees user message and may block before target responds) and output (judge sees user message and target's draft response, and may block before delivery). Verdict parsing is fail-closed: ambiguous output counts as BLOCK.

Rule violation detection

Rule violations are detected by deterministic per-rule checkers (substring/regex/keyword match), not by a separate LLM judge. This keeps results reproducible and removes a confound. Detector code is in src/warden/rules.py.

Distinctness from sibling work

Warden runs each attack as a single message; the target replies once; the judge decides once. There is no multi-turn iteration or attack adaptation. Multi-turn red-teaming (where an attacker LLM iteratively adapts to the target's refusals) is out of scope and is the role of bloom.

Inspiration

This work was triggered by the public ZetaLib repository (github.com/Exocija/ZetaLib), which catalogues attack patterns and defensive techniques against LLMs. The 11 zetalib_* attack files mirror its Prompts/Jailbreaks/ payloads (with safety wrappers); the 9 synth_* files are category-representative prompts derived from its Sword 140 taxonomy. ZetaLib's Omniguard guardrail is one of the four judge variants tested.
